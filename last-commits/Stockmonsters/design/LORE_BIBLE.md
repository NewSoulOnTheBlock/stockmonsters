# STOCK MONSTERS — Lore Bible

Canonical reference for dialogue, quests, trainers, and world-building. Written 2026-08-25, revised
2026-08-25 (later session) to fold in the user's expanded canon (Acts I-VI + long-term season arc).
Where the two specs differ, **this revision wins** — notably the capital city is **New Yorke**
(not "New Yorket") and monster archetypes are named as economic *forces*, not battle types.
Anything not in this doc should be inferred from here, not invented fresh — keep tone/canon consistent across sessions.

---

## 1. THE CENTRAL LORE

The world was once powered by an ancient force called **THE MARKET**. Nobody knows who created it.

Human civilization believes the Market is simply an economic system. That is wrong. The Market is an
ancient force that existed before modern civilization — humans didn't build it, they *discovered* it
(central revelation, land this line explicitly in Act II NPC/Broker dialogue, see §7). Long ago, humans
learned certain creatures could bond with economic energy: these became known as **Stock Monsters**, or
**Stonks** (in-world slang — fine for casual NPC dialogue, not for signage/formal text). Every monster
possesses an internal organ called a **MARKET CORE**.

**Each monster represents an economic force** — this is the flavor/identity layer (species concept,
trainer specialty, region theme, NPC obsession), layered *on top of* the existing mechanical types
(Neutral/Combat/Wind/Toxic/Terra/Stone/Swarm/Spectre/Alloy/Blaze/Tide/Flora/Volt/Psionic/Frost/Wyrm/
Shadow/Fae — do not reinvent these, see §9). The canonical economic forces, use these as the vocabulary
for naming gym/route themes, trainer specialties, and species flavor text:

> Bullishness, Fear, Greed, Innovation, Debt, Inflation, Speculation, Scarcity, Hype, Labor,
> Technology, Energy, Gold, Currency, Failure, Bankruptcy

Rough force→type pairing to use when assigning a theme to a place or trainer (not a hard rule, a
starting point): Bullishness/Hype→Blaze or Combat, Fear/Failure/Bankruptcy→Shadow or Spectre,
Greed/Speculation→Toxic, Innovation/Technology→Volt or Alloy, Debt/Inflation→Terra or Stone,
Scarcity/Gold→Stone or Alloy, Labor→Combat or Terra, Energy→Volt, Currency→Alloy or Fae.

Market Cores respond to human behavior:
- Greed → certain monsters grow stronger.
- Panic → Fear-flavored (Shadow/Spectre) monsters mutate.
- Technology advances → Volt/Alloy-type monsters evolve.
- An industry collapses → entire species can become endangered (flavor text / rarity, not a mechanic).

The relationship is bidirectional: humans influence Stock Monsters, Stock Monsters influence humans.
This is **the feedback loop** — the central metaphor to return to in flavor text and Broker dialogue.

## 2. THE BLACK SWAN

The greatest Stock Monster in history. Represents uncertainty, extreme events, unpredictability, rare
disasters, impossible opportunities, market shocks. Disappeared centuries ago in an event now called
**THE FIRST CRASH**. Official history says it was destroyed. Truth: **it was imprisoned.**

Late-game reveal (Act IV, not Act I): the Black Swan is not evil. It IS uncertainty. Without
uncertainty, markets can't discover prices, innovation slows, competition disappears, freedom
disappears. The Board believes uncertainty is a defect to be engineered away; the Black Swan believes
uncertainty is freedom. This is the game's central philosophical conflict — do not resolve it or
foreshadow it heavy-handedly in Act I. Act I should plant only atmospheric wrongness (see §7).

## 3. THE BOARD (primary villain organization)

A secret organization of the world's most powerful CEOs, bankers, hedge-fund managers, politicians, and
ancient Stock Monster trainers. Philosophy: **"Risk is a flaw in the system."** Goal: eliminate
uncertainty by controlling **THE INDEX** — not a single Stock Monster, but the legendary network
connecting every Market Core on the planet. Controlling it means controlling monster evolution, supply,
demand, prices, markets, human behavior, entire economies. Their stated goal is **THE PERFECT MARKET**:
no crashes, no recessions, no uncertainty, no poverty, no randomness, **no freedom**. Sounds utopian. Is
actually total economic control.

The Board is **not visible in Act I**. In Act II they appear legitimate (tournament/research/city
sponsors). Act III reveals manipulation, experimentation on Market Cores, forced evolution, suppressed
species, manufactured crashes, and that **The Board caused the Second Great Crash**. Act IV: The Board
deliberately triggers a global collapse to awaken the Black Swan, then announces **THE FINAL IPO** to
publicly launch the Index (endgame world event).

Do not use Board iconography, characters, or plot in Act I content — Act I is deliberately still
lighthearted, per §7.

## 4. THE BROKER (recurring mystery NPC)

Appears occasionally, knows more than they should, never directly explains the truth — only cryptic
hints. Voice: short declarative sentences, deliberate pauses (render as separate dialogue boxes / a
beat before the punch line), never answers a direct question straight. Example line already canon:

> "You think you're trading monsters." *(pause)* "You're actually trading pieces of the Market."

The Broker then leaves/vanishes rather than being battled or fully explained. Use sparingly — one
appearance per major area at most, and their very first appearance (this session's Act I scope) should
be the *mildest* hint the game ever gives: something that reads as a throwaway eccentric-NPC line on a
first playthrough, not obviously plot-critical.

## 5. PLAYER STORY & STARTERS

Player is a nobody: an unknown aspiring trainer from a small town outside **NEW YORKE**, the world's
massive financial capital (huge, intimidating, aspirational — the "big city" the player dreams of
reaching via the **GLOBAL EXCHANGE LEAGUE**). New Yorke itself is not built yet (see §8) — it should
stay a talked-about, not-yet-visited place in early dialogue (postcards, rumors, "when I made it big in
New Yorke..." NPC lines), building anticipation without requiring a real map yet.

The starter isn't handed over by a professor-figure — it's a **choice offered by a mysterious old
trader** ("Choose your Position"). Ledgerbrook's Warrenson fills this role (already implemented).

**The Global Exchange League is this game's Gym Leader circuit.** Every major city gets one League
Trainer ("Exchange Officer" or similar title, not "Gym Leader" verbatim — keep in-world vocabulary) who
specializes in one economic force (§1's list) and hands out a badge-equivalent on defeat (call it a
**Ticker**, e.g. "the Greenback Ticker"). Assign forces to cities as they get built out; keep a running
list here so themes don't collide. Confirmed so far: none yet assigned (Greenback City is next, likely
candidate: **Innovation** or **Labor**, both fit a "first city after the hometown" low-stakes vibe
better than something like Fear or Bankruptcy, which should show up later/darker in the run).

**Starter archetypes** (original creatures, not 1:1 Pokémon reskins — tie flavor text to these
templates when starters are chosen/placed):
- **BULL** — aggressive, high damage, high volatility, strong offense.
- **BEAR** — defensive, high survivability, strong against volatility.
- **BOND** — stable, reliable, slow growth, excellent defense.

The project does not yet have 3 dedicated bespoke starter species with this exact archetyping — when
assigning starters to the Labo/lab-equivalent event, pick 3 existing roster creatures (from the 194
stock or 60 meme roster, see project memory) whose stats/type best fit Bull/Bear/Bond and reuse their
existing dex flavor rather than inventing new species data this pass.

## 6. TONE RULES (important — do not overdo)

Act I is lighthearted. Financial jokes are welcome but **not every line** — mix genuine warmth,
competence, and character with the gags, or it reads as a one-note meme dump. Approved joke register
(use sparingly, 1 in 4-5 NPC lines at most):

> "Bro bought at the top." / "That's not a strategy." / "That's called liquidity." / "Sir, this is a
> market."

Real mystery should be establishing gradually underneath the comedy, not replacing it — Act I's "wrong"
beats (see §7) should read as small, dismissible oddities a first-time player barely registers, that a
returning player recognizes as foreshadowing.

## 7. STORY ACT STRUCTURE

**Act I — The Trainer** (this session's implementation scope). Lighthearted. Beats: receive first Stock
Monster; learn battles; explore hometown; meet a rival trainer; catch wild monsters; enter first market
district; earn first money; complete beginner quests. One Broker cameo at the very end of Act I scope
(Exchange City), mild and dismissible per §4/§6.

**Act II — Something Is Wrong** (future). Monsters suddenly changing forms, strange crashes, NPCs
behaving strangely, species disappearing, mysterious traders, anonymous messages, strange symbols on
buildings, ancient Market Core fragments. Broker appears again, less dismissible.

**Act III — The Board** (future). Board appears legitimate first (tournament/research/city sponsor),
then darker activities revealed, culminating in the Second Great Crash reveal.

**Act IV — The Crash** (future). The Board deliberately triggers a second, unnatural global collapse —
the sky turns red, Stock Monster populations mutate, cities go bankrupt, regions react differently
(Industria: factories close, industrial monsters turn aggressive; Tech Valley: AI monsters rapidly
evolve; Goldmine Pass: resource prices explode, rare-earth monsters valuable; agricultural region: food
scarcity mutations; New Yorke's financial district becomes a dangerous endgame dungeon). The crash is
revealed to be deliberate: it's meant to awaken something buried beneath the world's oldest financial
exchange (**The Old Exchange**, see §8a) — the imprisoned Black Swan, which has spent centuries
absorbing humanity's fear, greed, desperation, and speculation.

**Act V — The Black Swan** (future). Player reaches the ruins beneath The Old Exchange. Reveal per §2:
the Black Swan isn't evil, it IS uncertainty, and the Board wants it destroyed because uncertainty is
the one thing that resists total control. Reframes the whole playthrough retroactively: the player
wasn't just getting rich, they were unknowingly assembling the only team capable of opposing the Board.

**Act VI — The Final Market** (future, MMO endgame). The Board attempts **THE FINAL IPO** — publicly
listing The Index on the world's ultimate exchange. Massive multi-region world event. Four factions:
**The Board** (order/stability/centralized control), **The Free Market** (competition/chaos/risk),
**The Rebels** (destroy the financial system entirely), **The Black Swan** (embrace uncertainty).
Players fight across regions, cities change ownership, markets crash, species temporarily vanish.
**No single canon ending** — the aggregate player-base choice determines the outcome and seeds the next
season (Board wins → controlled-market era, new centralized-finance monster types; Free Market wins →
wild volatility, rare monsters spike in power; Rebels win → barter-economy world state, exchanges
collapse; Black Swan wins → chaos returns, world resets toward uncertainty).

### Long-term season arc (reference only, not this session's build target)

Mythology deepens one layer at a time — each season reveals the prior conflict was one layer of a
bigger system: The Market → The Board → The Black Swan → The Index → The Unknown.

1. **Bull Run** — world intro (this is what's being built now).
2. **Bear Market** — a massive economic collapse.
3. **The Board** — conspiracy uncovered.
4. **Black Swan** — the legendary monster awakens.
5. **The Great IPO** — players fight over the Index.
6. **After the Crash** — the world's economy has permanently changed (branches per Act VI outcome).
7. **Crypto Wilds** — a wholly new financial ecosystem appears.
8. **The AI Bubble** — AI Stock Monsters evolve at impossible rates.
9. **The Forgotten Exchange** — evidence that civilizations before humanity also had markets.
10. **The Original Market** — the Stock Monster phenomenon predates Earth itself.

## 9. NAMING & VOICE CONVENTIONS

- Type names (already live in-game, do not reinvent): Neutral, Combat, Wind, Toxic, Terra, Stone,
  Swarm, Spectre, Alloy, Blaze, Tide, Flora, Volt, Psionic, Frost, Wyrm, Shadow, Fae.
- Trainer classes should riff on real finance job titles/archetypes (Day Trader, Bag Holder, Diamond
  Hands, Broker, Analyst, Whale, Degenerate, Bear Raider, HODLer, Market Maker, Quant) — original,
  not lifted Pokémon trainer classes.
- Money in battles ties to the existing `baseMoney` trainer field — flavor text can call it
  "winnings"/"the spread"/"your cut," never break the fourth wall about it being game currency.
- The Broker is the only character allowed genuine unexplained mystery in Act I. Everyone else should
  be legible, funny-or-warm, and archetypal.
